The regime gate

Why frost forecasts can use terrain that interpolation cannot

HALM — height above local minimum — was rejected as an interpolation covariate because on top of the spline it bought 0.5%, and the daily fitted coefficient had the right sign on only 57% of days. That 57% is not noise. It is two physical regimes averaged into one number.

On radiative nights — calm, clear, high pressure, dry air — cold air drains and pools, and a hollow runs 5–8 °C below the slope above it. Terrain dominates completely. On advective nights — a cold airmass arriving with wind — the boundary layer mixes, nothing pools, and the field is smooth. Terrain is close to irrelevant.

An interpolator sees only observations. It cannot tell which night it is, so it fits one national coefficient across both and lands at 57%. A forecast has wind, cloud and dewpoint as inputs. It knows the regime before the night happens, so it can switch models.

The second measurement that matters is from this week's per-region run. Upper Wairau's tmin bias moved −0.967 → +0.035 °C when its station count went 7 → 15. Lower Wairau −0.548 → +0.050 at 7 → 10. The frost-valley warm bias tracks station count, not model skill. Frost was parked as an unfixable sub-grid limit; it is substantially a coverage problem.

What this reframes

Part of a frost product is a thermometer deployment programme, not just code. That is also the part a competitor cannot copy — and it is the same lever that improves the surfaces we already sell.

Licensing, before any build

MetService's open-access page says its listed data is "freely available for personal, professional or commercial use", subject to clause 4 of its terms. A frost alert derived from their fields is squarely a commercial derived product. Resolve the derived-product question in writing before it becomes load-bearing — the ECMWF-only fallback changes the architecture if the answer is no.

Two ingest traps worth naming now

The clock does not switch. There is no duplicated or missing hour at the April DST boundary, and the daylight midpoint is flat across it — so the logger runs a fixed offset, and the geometry puts that at UTC+13 rather than UTC+12. In winter it is therefore running an hour ahead of NZ local time. For a frost station this is not cosmetic: Tmin falls near dawn, so a one-hour shift walks the minimum across the day boundary — the same class of error as the CLIFLO 09:00–09:00 offset we already decompose for. Confirm the offset with the operator rather than trusting my inference from solar geometry; a mountain horizon biases the midpoint and I would not stake a published number on it.

The file is year-to-date, not an archive. It starts 1 January. History before 2026 has to be requested separately, and the station has run since 2016 — that is a decade of high-country radiation data worth asking for in the same conversation.

Why the longwave matters more than the thermometer

Almost nothing in our network measures incoming longwave. It is the direct physical driver of radiative frost, which means Pisa is not just another station in the fit — it is the site where the regime classifier can be validated against the actual radiative forcing rather than against a wind-and-cloud proxy. One station cannot train a national classifier, but it can tell you whether yours is measuring the right thing.

The page states the data "has not been subject to quality assurance". Our daily_qc stage already exists and runs before the spline; Pisa should enter through it like every other source, with the pinned-value and DTR-cap checks applied. It should not get a pass for being a research station.

Reuse and the new

Most of this is already in production — one schema change is not

The publish and serve chain transfers wholesale. fastgrid does the downscale, raster.py writes the LERC COG, surface_store.py renders tiles from the private bucket, the index scripts populate surface_run. A forecast is, structurally, just another granularity in the v2 contract.

Except in one place, and it is worth seeing rather than reading.

What transfers unchanged

  • fastgrid basis, raster.py LERC writer, surface_store.py tile rendering, the private-bucket IAM path — all proven in production.
  • daily_qc and the quarantine machinery, which any new station source must pass through.
  • Expo push, UnifiedEmailService and the notification bell — the entire alert delivery layer already exists.
  • The 10,379-cell planted mask, which gives block-level forecasts for Grow customers for free.

What is genuinely new

  • NWP ingest on cycle times — a different cadence and a different failure mode from anything we run. A late cycle has a deadline attached.
  • The (issued_at, valid_at, lead) key and the migration above.
  • The verification store, and it must exist from day one.
  • The regime classifier — the only new science.
  • Terrain derivatives at 1 m, which is a real compute job over 90% of the mainland.

The verification loop

The structural difference between this and the era-offset field

We have built a learned correction field once already, and it worked — the era offset cut held-out RMSE 43% on 2023 and 48% on 2024. It is also permanently frozen, because CLIFLO closed in October 2024 and there will never be another overlap month. As the DB network grows, that frozen field increasingly over-corrects, and the error grows silently because there is no reference left to measure it against.

Ship the product probabilistic and calibrated. A grower deciding whether to start fans at 02:00 wants P(T < −1 °C at 05:00), not a point value. Publish reliability diagrams and Brier scores. A single number that misses one frost destroys credibility permanently; a calibrated 30% that verifies at 30% survives being wrong.

Neither of the two comparable NZ services publishes a skill score. weatherstation.net.nz offers 48-hour, 7-day and 14-day frost outlooks with SMS, email and app alerts off its own station network, and claims "unprecedented accuracy" with nothing behind it. Open verification numbers would be a genuine differentiator in this market, not merely good practice.

Build order

Phase 0 is the decision point and costs almost nothing

  1. 00Phase

    Prove the regime split

    days · no new data · no spend

    Take the six-year DB archive. Classify each night radiative or advective from observed wind, a cloud proxy and dewpoint depression. Refit the HALM coefficient separately on each subset.

    The test: does sign agreement go from 57% to 85%+ on the calm subset? If yes, the thesis holds and you know the ceiling before spending a dollar on an API. If no, stop here — and you have lost a week, not a quarter.

  2. 01Phase

    Cold-pool prior from night LST

    2–3 weeks · free data · improves today's product

    Composite VIIRS night-time LST anomaly over clear calm nights into a static 500 m frost-proneness raster. Validate against the frost-hollow stations we already have.

    This is the part I would do regardless of whether the forecast ships. It is an observed substitute for the sub-grid term the spline cannot recover, and it improves the interpolated surfaces we sell today. LST is skin temperature and clear-sky-only by construction — it is a covariate, never a measurement.

  3. 02Phase

    Forecast ingest and MOS at stations only

    the real build · ECMWF first, no contract needed

    Stand up NWP ingest and fit the MOS at the 259 station points. No grid, no tiles, no product. Verification store from the first forecast — every lead time, every station, joined to what happened.

    Prove skill against two baselines stated upfront: raw NWP Tmin, and persistence. If we cannot beat those at points, a 500 m grid of the same model is just a prettier way to be wrong.

  4. 03Phase

    Grid, probability, alerts

    reuses the whole publish chain

    Downscale to 500 m through the existing fastgrid basis. Add the (issued_at, valid_at, lead) migration and the contract amendment. Ship P(frost) at −2 / 0 / +2 °C, hours below zero, and time of first crossing. Wire alerts through Expo push and the existing email service, block-level off the planted mask.

    Add Himawari for evening nowcast updates once the base product verifies.

  5. 04Phase

    Densify the hollows — Central Otago first

    ongoing · hardware, not software

    Upper Wairau proved the return: bias −0.967 → +0.035 for eight thermometers. Central Otago has six temperature stations and Bannockburn has none, in the region where frost decides the vintage.

    Probe ORC HydroTel in parallel — it needs only an HTTP Basic account and we have never run the probe.

Risks and gaps

The things most likely to make this fail

Central Otago is the product risk

It is the worst zone we have on every variable and the most frost-critical region in the country. A frost product that is weak there is weak exactly where it is bought. That gap is a siting problem and it should close before launch, not after.

The 500 m grid is a ceiling on the output

Cold pooling operates at 100 m to 1 km. A frost hollow can be narrower than one of our cells, so even a perfect model has a resolution floor. The 1 m LINZ LiDAR fixes the terrain predictors; it does not on its own change the grid we publish on. Whether the frost product needs its own finer grid over growing regions is a genuine open question and I would not answer it before Phase 2.

Liability

A missed frost costs a grower a crop. Advisory framing, calibrated probabilities rather than point values, and terms that say so — settled early, not bolted on at launch.

Licensing

MetService derived-product rights, and attribution obligations on ECMWF (CC-BY), LINZ (CC-BY) and Pisa. We already carry the LENZ CC-BY attribution in-band in the surface manifests, so the mechanism exists.

What I could not settle from here

  • The Pisa clock offset. Fixed rather than DST-switching is proven; UTC+13 specifically is inferred from solar geometry at a site with a mountain horizon. Ask the operator.
  • Pisa history before 2026. The served CSV is year-to-date. The station has run since 2016.
  • MetService pricing at our volume. The unit model is requests × variables × points; a national grid request pattern could price very differently from a per-site one.
  • Whether HortPlus is a competitor or a supplier. They have 100+ research-grade stations, 20 years of history, an API, and they already accept Harvest hardware — which we also read. Worth a conversation before assuming either.
